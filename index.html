How it decides

  1. Finds the 1% line. EU and US labels list ingredients above 1% in descending order; those at 1% or less follow in any order. Phenoxyethanol, benzyl alcohol and potassium sorbate are legally capped at or below 1% in the EU, so everything after them is at most 1%. Fragrance usually is too but isn't capped, so a line drawn there is marked "likely".
  2. Rates each ingredient for Malassezia. Based on lab studies that grew Malassezia species on cosmetic ingredients. Results are weighted toward M. globosa and M. restricta, which dominate on flaky scalps, so growth by M. furfur alone counts as low.
  3. Flags allergy and irritation separately. EU-listed fragrance allergens, strong preservative sensitisers, formaldehyde releasers, drying alcohol and harsh surfactants.
  4. Combines them into a verdict. High: a high-risk ingredient above the 1% line. Moderate: a moderate one above the line, or a high one below it. Low: only weak flags. Minimal: none. Rinse-off products drop one step for shorter contact; that step is a judgement, not a study result.

Sources

Limits: nearly all of this evidence comes from yeast grown on lab plates, mostly M. furfur and a few other species. No clinical trial has tested how styling products affect seborrhoeic dermatitis. People differ: in DeAngelis 2005, applied oleic acid caused flaking only in dandruff-prone people. Ingredients the database doesn't know are listed, not guessed. Formulas change, so check the current label. This is not medical advice; a dermatologist can patch-test you for allergies.
